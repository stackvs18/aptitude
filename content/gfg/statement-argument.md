## What it is
A statement (usually "Should …?") is followed by arguments for and against. Decide which arguments are **strong**.

## Strong vs weak arguments
**Strong:**
- Directly related to the statement.
- Backed by facts or a universally accepted truth.
- Points out a real, important consequence.

**Weak:**
- Opinion, emotion or a vague generalisation.
- Superficial or simplistic ("it's good for everyone").
- Ambiguous, or only loosely linked to the question.
- Uses "only", "all" or "always" without support.

## Example
Should mobile phones be banned in schools?
- I. Yes, they distract students from studies. → **Strong.**
- II. No, all students like phones. → **Weak** (liking isn't a reason).

## Tips
- Judge each argument separately; both "yes" and "no" arguments can be strong.
- Don't let your own opinion decide.
