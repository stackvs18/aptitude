## Formula
**A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ** and **CI = A − P**, where interest is added to the principal at the end of every period.
*Example:* ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years → A = 10000 × 1.1² = 12,100 → CI = **₹2,100**.

## Derivation
Year 1: P(1 + R/100). Year 2 earns interest on that whole amount: P(1 + R/100)². After n years: P(1 + R/100)ⁿ.

## Compounding more often
| Compounded | Rate per period | Periods |
|---|---|---|
| Yearly | R | n |
| Half-yearly | R/2 | 2n |
| Quarterly | R/4 | 4n |
| Monthly | R/12 | 12n |
| Daily | R/365 | 365n |

General: **A = P(1 + r/(100k))^(kn)** for k compoundings a year.

## Different rates each year
Rates R₁, R₂, R₃ → A = P(1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100)(1 + R₃/100).

## Rule of 72
Years to **double** money ≈ 72 ÷ rate. At 8%: about **9 years**.

## CI vs SI shortcuts
- **2 years:** CI − SI = **P(R/100)²**.
- **3 years:** CI − SI = **P(R/100)²(3 + R/100)**.
- *Example:* P = 5000, R = 10%, 2 years → difference = 5000 × 0.01 = **₹50**.

## Compound interest of consecutive years
Amounts in consecutive years are in GP. If it is A₁ after year n and A₂ after year n + 1, then R = (A₂ − A₁)/A₁ × 100.
A sum becomes x times in n years → it becomes x^k times in kn years (doubles in 5 → 4 times in 10).
