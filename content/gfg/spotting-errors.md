## What it is
A sentence is split into parts (a), (b), (c), (d). Pick the part with a grammatical error, or "no error".

## Rules that catch most errors
- **Subject–verb agreement:** singular subject → singular verb. "Each of the boys **has**", "The quality of the mangoes **is** good", "Neither he nor I **am**" (the verb agrees with the nearer subject).
- **Pronoun case:** "between you and **me**", "He is taller than **I** (am)".
- **Tense consistency:** "He said that he **was** busy" (not "is"). "If I **were** you…".
- **Articles:** "**an** hour", "**a** university", "**the** Ganga".
- **Prepositions:** "married **to**", "senior **to**", "prefer tea **to** coffee", "comprised **of**" is wrong (use "comprises" or "is composed of").
- **Comparatives:** "more better" ✗ → "better"; "senior/junior/superior/inferior" take **to**, not "than".
- **Redundancy:** "return back", "repeat again" and "the reason is because" are wrong.
- **"One of the" + plural noun**: "one of the best **players**".
- **Lest** is followed by **should**: "Run fast lest you **should** miss the bus."
- **Hardly/scarcely … when; no sooner … than.**

## Sample
"Neither of the two candidates / are suitable / for the post / no error." → error in (b): **is** suitable.

## Tip
Read the whole sentence first, then check the verb, then pronouns, articles and prepositions.
