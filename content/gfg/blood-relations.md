## Types of questions
1. **Pointing/introducing:** "Pointing to a man, Rita said, 'He is the son of my father's only son.'"
2. **Family tree:** several statements; draw the tree and answer questions.
3. **Coded relations:** "A + B means A is the brother of B; A × B means A is the father of B …"
4. **Mixed relations:** a combination of the above.
5. **Puzzle-based:** a family puzzle combined with seating or professions.
6. **Single-person:** describing one person through a chain ("my mother's brother's son").

## Tips
1. **Start from the speaker ("me")** and work backwards from the end of the sentence.
2. **Check every option.** "Brother or cousin" style options exist for a reason.
3. **Don't assume gender from names.** Only use stated words (son, sister, he, she).
4. **"Only son" or "only daughter" of my parent** usually means the speaker (if the gender matches).
5. Identify exactly which **two people** the question asks about.
6. Correlate each given relation with the next in order.
7. **Draw a diagram**.

## Relation terms
| Relation | Means |
|---|---|
| Father's / mother's father | Grandfather |
| Father's brother | Uncle (paternal) |
| Mother's brother | Maternal uncle |
| Father's / mother's sister | Aunt |
| Uncle's / aunt's child | Cousin |
| Brother's / sister's son (daughter) | Nephew (niece) |
| Son's wife | Daughter-in-law |
| Daughter's husband | Son-in-law |
| Wife's / husband's brother; sister's husband | Brother-in-law |
| Wife's / husband's sister; brother's wife | Sister-in-law |

## Diagram symbols
Male: □ or +. Female: ○ or −. Married couple: = or a double line. Siblings: a horizontal line. Parent → child: a vertical line. Each generation sits on its own row.

## Example
"Pointing to a photograph, a man said, 'I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son.'" → my father's son = me → the photo is of **my son**.
