## Formulas
- Present age x → after n years: **x + n**; n years ago: **x − n**.
- Ages in ratio p : q → take them as **px and qx**.
- If the present age is x, then n times the age is nx and 1/n of it is x/n.
- **The difference between two people's ages never changes.**
- Sum of ages of a group grows by (number of people) × n after n years.

## Shortcut for ratio questions
A : B = 3 : 2 now and 4 : 3 after 10 years.
1. The difference A − B is fixed, so make the differences of both ratios equal: 3 : 2 (diff 1) and 4 : 3 (diff 1). They already match.
2. A went from 3 parts to 4 parts → 1 part = 10 years.
3. So A = 30, B = 20 now.

If the differences don't match, scale one ratio. *Example:* 5 : 3 now and 3 : 2 after 6 years → diffs 2 and 1 → make the second 6 : 4. A went 5 → 6 parts, 1 part = 6 years → A = 30, B = 18.

## Solved examples
- **Father/son:** Father is 4 times his son's age. In 20 years he'll be twice the son's age. Let son = x: 4x + 20 = 2(x + 20) → x = 10 → son 10, father **40**.
- **Sum + ratio:** sum of ages is 50; 5 years ago ratio was 1 : 3 → (x + 5) + (3x + 5) = 50 → x = 10 → ages **15 and 35**.
- **Average age:** a class of 30 averages 14 years; with the teacher it's 15 → teacher = 31 × 15 − 30 × 14 = **45**.

## Tips
- Read "x years ago" vs "after x years" carefully, and put both people at the same time point before forming the equation.
- Back-solving from the options is often fastest.
