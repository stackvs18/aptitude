## What it is
Given a statement, decide which **conclusions** definitely follow from it, using only the information given.

## Concepts
- A conclusion must be **directly supported** by the statement.
- It can't add new facts or rely on general knowledge.
- Words like "only", "all", "always" and "never" make a conclusion too strong unless the statement says the same.

## Example
Statement: "The government has increased the number of scholarships for girls' higher education."
- I. More girls may go for higher education. → **Follows.**
- II. Boys don't need scholarships. → **Doesn't follow.**

## Tips and tricks
- Read the statement twice and underline the key claim.
- Reject conclusions that are opposites, extreme, or about something not mentioned.
- Two contradictory conclusions can't both follow, but "either I or II" applies when exactly one must be true.
