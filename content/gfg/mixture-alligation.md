## Mixture
A mixture combines two or more ingredients. Two kinds:
- **Simple mixture:** two ingredients (milk + water).
- **Compound mixture:** two or more mixtures mixed together.
**Mean price** = cost price of one unit of the mixture.

## Alligation rule
To mix a cheaper ingredient (price c) with a dearer one (price d) to get mean price m:

**Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)**

```
   c (cheaper)        d (dearer)
          \            /
           m (mean price)
          /            \
     (d − m)          (m − c)
```

The same rule works for concentrations, profit percentages, speeds, interest rates and average marks.
*Example:* rice at ₹15 and ₹20 to make ₹16.50 → (20 − 16.5) : (16.5 − 15) = 3.5 : 1.5 = **7 : 3**.

## Replacement formula
A vessel holds x litres of liquid. y litres are taken out and replaced by water, n times:
**Liquid left = x(1 − y/x)ⁿ.**
*Example:* 40 L milk, remove 4 L and refill with water 3 times → 40 × (0.9)³ = **29.16 L**.

## Tips and tricks
- Milkman sells at cost price but adds water and gains g% → **water : milk = g : 100**.
- To change a mixture's ratio by adding one ingredient, keep the other ingredient's quantity fixed and solve.
- Always write the alligation cross; don't do it in your head.
