## What it is
Reaching a **conclusion that must be true** from given premises (deductive reasoning), most often with syllogisms like "All A are B".

## Key parts
- **Premises:** the given statements.
- **Conclusion:** what follows necessarily.
- **Validity:** the conclusion must hold in every possible case consistent with the premises.

## Syllogism rules (with Venn diagrams)
- All A are B + All B are C → All A are C ✔.
- All A are B + Some B are C → nothing definite about A and C.
- Some A are B → Some B are A ✔ (converse is valid).
- No A is B → No B is A ✔.
- All A are B → Some A are B ✔, and Some B are A ✔.
- Two "some" premises → no definite conclusion.
- "Either–or" appears when two conclusions are complementary (Some A are C / No A is C) and neither follows alone.

## Example
All dogs are animals. Some animals are pets.
- I. Some dogs are pets. → **Doesn't follow** (possible, not certain).
- II. All dogs are animals. → **Follows.**

## Tips
Draw the minimum-overlap Venn diagram. If a conclusion can fail in any diagram, it doesn't follow.
