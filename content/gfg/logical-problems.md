## What it is
Short statements (usually three) where the third's truth must be judged from the first two: **true, false or uncertain**.

## How to solve
1. Put the items in order, writing the relations as "A > B".
2. Combine the statements.
3. Check whether the third statement must follow (true), contradicts them (false) or can't be decided (uncertain).

## Solved examples
- Tom runs faster than Jack. Jack runs faster than Sam. "Tom runs faster than Sam." → **True**.
- All the trees in the park are flowering trees. Some trees in the park are dogwoods. "All dogwoods in the park are flowering trees." → **True**.
- Apples cost more than oranges. Apples cost less than bananas. "Bananas cost less than oranges." → **False** (bananas > apples > oranges).
- A is taller than B; C is taller than B. "A is taller than C." → **Uncertain**.

## Tip
Don't use outside knowledge. Only what's given counts.
