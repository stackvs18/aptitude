## ① The idea
Average = Sum ÷ Count. **Almost every average problem becomes easy if you convert averages to totals.**

## ② Formulas
- Sum = Average × Number.
- Average of consecutive numbers / AP = (first + last)/2 = middle term.
- First n naturals: (n + 1)/2. First n even: n + 1. First n odd: n.
- **Replacement:** new person's value = old value + (number × change in average).
- **New entry:** value of newcomer = new avg + (old count × increase).
- **Average speed** (equal distances) = 2xy/(x + y). Three equal distances: 3xyz/(xy + yz + zx).
- Combined average = (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂)/(n₁ + n₂).

## ③ Worked examples
**Ex 1 (overlap).** Average of 11 results = 60. First 6 avg = 58, last 6 avg = 63. 6th result? It is counted twice: 6 × 58 + 6 × 63 − 11 × 60 = 348 + 378 − 660 = **66**.

**Ex 2 (cricket).** A batsman scores 87 in the 17th innings and his average rises by 3. New average? Let old avg = x: 16x + 87 = 17(x + 3) → x = 36 → new = **39**.
Shortcut: New avg = runs − (old innings × increase) = 87 − 16 × 3 = **39**.

**Ex 3 (replacement).** 8 men's average weight rises by 2.5 kg when one man of 65 kg is replaced. New man = 65 + 8 × 2.5 = **85 kg**.

**Ex 4 (teacher).** 30 students avg 15 yrs; with teacher avg becomes 16 → teacher = 31 × 16 − 30 × 15 = **46**.
Shortcut: teacher = new avg + (old count × increase) = 16 + 30 × 1 = 46.

**Ex 5 (family).** Avg of father & mother = 35; with son avg = 27 → son = 81 − 70 = **11**.

**Ex 6 (consecutive).** Average of 5 consecutive odd numbers = 61 → numbers 57, 59, 61, 63, 65.

**Ex 7 (speed).** Up at 40, down at 60 → 2 × 40 × 60/100 = **48 km/h**.

## ⑤ Traps
- "25a + 25b = 115, average of a and b" → a + b = 4.6, average = **2.3**, not 4.6.
- If all values increase by k, the average increases by k; if multiplied by k, the average is multiplied by k.
