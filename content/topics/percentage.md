## ① The idea
Percent = "per hundred". x% of y = xy/100. **Always ask: percent of WHAT?** The base is everything.

## ② Formulas
- x% of y = y% of x (so 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4).
- A is what % of B = (A/B) × 100.
- % change = (change / original) × 100.
- If A is r% **more** than B → B is **r/(100 + r) × 100 %** less than A.
- If A is r% **less** than B → B is **r/(100 − r) × 100 %** more than A.
- **Price rises by r% → cut consumption by r/(100 + r) × 100 %** to keep spending the same. Price falls r% → can increase consumption by r/(100 − r) × 100 %.
- **Successive changes a% and b%** → net = a + b + ab/100 (use negative sign for decrease).
- **Population/depreciation:** P(1 ± r/100)ⁿ. Population n years ago = P/(1 + r/100)ⁿ.
- **Election:** margin of victory = (winner% − loser%) of valid votes.
- **Exam:** pass marks = (marks got + marks short) = pass% of maximum.

## ③ Worked examples
**Ex 1.** Price of sugar rises 25%. Reduce consumption by 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.

**Ex 2.** A's salary is 20% less than B's. B's is how much more than A's? 20/80 × 100 = **25%**.

**Ex 3.** Increase 20% then decrease 20%: 20 − 20 − 400/100 = **−4%** (net loss 4%).

**Ex 4.** Population 10000 grows 10% p.a. After 2 yrs: 10000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = **12100**.

**Ex 5 (exam).** Need 33% to pass. Student gets 125 and fails by 40 → pass = 165 = 33% → max = **500**.

**Ex 6 (exam, two-percent).** Student A gets 30% and fails by 10; student B gets 40% and gets 15 more than pass. Max marks: 10% = 25 → max = 250, pass = 85.

**Ex 7 (election).** Winner 62%, loser 38%, margin 432 → 24% = 432 → total **1800**.

**Ex 8 (election with invalid).** 15200 votes, 15% invalid → valid 12920; loser got 45% → **5814**.

**Ex 9 (fraction-of-fraction).** After spending 20% on rent and 25% of the remainder on food, ₹2400 left. Salary? x × 0.8 × 0.75 = 2400 → x = **4000**.

## ④ Shortcuts
- Use the fraction table: "increase by 12.5%" = × 9/8; "decrease by 16⅔%" = × 5/6.
- 10% then 10% increase = 21%; 20% & 20% = 44%; 10% up & 10% down = −1%.

## ⑤ Traps
- "Percent less than" vs "percent of" — read twice.
- Percentage points vs percent: 10% → 12% is +2 percentage points but +20%.
