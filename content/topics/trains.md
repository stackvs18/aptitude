## ① The idea
A train has **length**. To "cross" something, the train must cover **its own length + the length of the thing**.

## ② Cases (memorise this table)

| Train crosses… | Distance to cover | Speed used |
|---|---|---|
| Pole / man standing / tree / signal | L (train) | S |
| Platform / bridge / tunnel / stationary train | L + P | S |
| Man walking at s (same dir.) | L | S − s |
| Man walking at s (opposite) | L | S + s |
| Another train (opposite dirs.) | L₁ + L₂ | S₁ + S₂ |
| Another train (same dir.) | L₁ + L₂ | S₁ − S₂ |

Two trains start from A and B towards each other; after meeting they take a and b hours to reach their destinations → **S_A : S_B = √b : √a**.

## ③ Worked examples
**Ex 1.** 280 m at 63 km/h past a tree → 63 × 5/18 = 17.5 m/s → 280/17.5 = **16 s**.

**Ex 2.** 120 m at 90 km/h over a 230 m platform → 350/25 = **14 s**.

**Ex 3.** Passes a pole in 20 s and a 100 m platform in 30 s → 10 s for 100 m → 10 m/s → length **200 m**.

**Ex 4.** Platform in 36 s, man in 20 s at 54 km/h (15 m/s) → train 300 m, platform 540 − 300 = **240 m**.

**Ex 5 (opposite).** 140 m and 160 m at 60 and 40 km/h → 300 m at 100 km/h (250/9 m/s) → **10.8 s**.

**Ex 6 (same direction).** Equal trains at 46 and 36 km/h; faster passes slower in 36 s → relative 10 km/h = 25/9 m/s × 36 = 100 m = 2L → **L = 50 m**.

**Ex 7 (man walking).** 125 m train passes a man walking at 5 km/h (same dir.) in 10 s → relative 12.5 m/s = 45 km/h → train = **50 km/h**.

**Ex 8 (√ rule).** After meeting, trains take 9 h and 16 h → speeds ratio √16 : √9 = **4 : 3**.

## ⑤ Trap
Train crossing a **man sitting in another train** → use both trains' speeds (relative), but distance = only the length of the train being crossed.
