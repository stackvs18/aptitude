## ① The idea
When two things of different prices (or concentrations) are mixed, the mixture's value lies **between** them, and it is closer to whichever is used more.

## ② The Alligation Rule (the "cross" diagram)

```
   Cheaper (c)            Dearer (d)
           \               /
            Mean price (m)
           /               \
      (d − m)            (m − c)
Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c)
```

Works for prices, % concentration, % profit, speeds, average marks, interest rates — **anything averaged**.

**Repeated replacement:** a container has x litres of liquid; y litres are taken out and replaced with water, n times. Liquid left = x(1 − y/x)ⁿ.

**Milkman formula:** gains g% by mixing water and selling at C.P. → water : milk = g : 100.

## ③ Worked examples
**Ex 1.** Rice ₹15 and ₹20 for mean ₹16.50 → (20 − 16.5) : (16.5 − 15) = 3.5 : 1.5 = **7 : 3**.

**Ex 2.** Water with milk at ₹12/L to get ₹8/L → water (₹0) : milk = (12 − 8) : (8 − 0) = **1 : 2**.

**Ex 3 (with profit).** Teas at ₹60 and ₹65, mixture sold at ₹68.20 with 10% gain → mean C.P. = 62 → (65 − 62) : (62 − 60) = **3 : 2**.

**Ex 4 (profit %).** 1000 kg sugar, part at 8% gain, rest at 18%, overall 14% → (18 − 14) : (14 − 8) = 2 : 3 → 18% part = **600 kg**.

**Ex 5 (replacement).** 40 L milk; 4 L removed & replaced by water, 3 times → 40 × (0.9)³ = **29.16 L**.

**Ex 6 (adding to reach a ratio).** 20 L mixture, milk : water = 3 : 1 → milk 15, water 5. To make 4 : 1, water stays 5 → milk must be 20 → add **5 L** milk.

**Ex 7 (fraction replaced).** 40% alcohol replaced partly by 19% alcohol → 26%. Original : replacing = (26 − 19) : (40 − 26) = 1 : 2 → replaced fraction = **2/3**.

## ⑤ Trap
Alligation gives the ratio **cheaper : dearer** in the order you wrote them. Write the diagram every time; never do it in your head.
